Palette candidates (SW-3635, round 2)

Every hex comes from the palette's own source (listed at the foot). Contrast is WCAG 2 relative luminance of each token colour against the theme's own background and against pure black (dark) or pure white (light), since a terminal's background is often pure black or white. Red = below 3:1, amber = below 4.5:1. Bold shows as a heavier weight only. Which named colour goes to which class is mine unless noted; hex values are from the sources.

Light or dark detection, and the choice

  • Needs detection: candidates 1, 2 and 3 (truecolor, with a separate dark and light set). Candidate 4 follows the user's own palette and needs none, but it inherits the palette's weak spots (yellow, green and cyan on white; blue on black).
  • OSC 11 (xterm control sequences): OSC 11 ; ? ST asks the terminal for its background colour (the document says: a ? instead of a colour specification makes xterm reply with the current value). Needs a terminal on both ends, raw-mode read with a timeout; a pager or pipe can race the reply. bat does exactly this through the terminal-colorsaurus crate, only when stdout is a terminal, and falls back to dark (ColorScheme defaults to Dark); users can force with BAT_THEME (dark/light/auto), and on macOS auto:system reads AppleInterfaceStyle.
  • COLORFGBG (set by rxvt-family terminals and a few others, form fg;bg): Vim uses it to guess background ("dark" when $COLORFGBG suggests a dark background; otherwise it defaults to "light" on non-Windows systems). Cheap and needs no I/O, but absent from most macOS terminals, so only a hint.
  • Fallback order I would use: an explicit user setting (environment variable or flag) first; then OSC 11 when stdin and stdout are terminals; then COLORFGBG; then dark.
  • My pick: Primer dark and light, with detection. It is the only candidate where every token class is 4.9:1 or better on its own background (and 4.9 or better on pure black and white, see the tables), it is MIT, and its dark set reads as colour without blue-on-black thin text (its numbers are a light blue, #79c0ff, 9.7:1 on black). Catppuccin Mocha is as pleasant on dark (every class 5.8 to 12.9), but Latte is weak on white (type, warning and operator 2.3 to 2.5, text 3.0), so it would need darker substitutes there; Atom One Light has a weak comment colour (2.5). If Matt would rather have no detection and nothing to maintain, take terminal-native with bold operators: it adapts to the user's own theme, but its contrast on white is poor for yellow, green and cyan (1.9 to 3.3) and on black for red (2.4 to 3.6), the reason the first round went to 256-colour mid-tones.
